# HydraCache 0.73.0 — Evidence-driven performance efficiency

Status: **shipped 2026-10-03** from exact candidate
`d1db9937e61295341ac95f289bace275641b1650`, tagged `v0.73.0`.

0.73 applies six narrowly scoped runtime improvements only after owner attribution, local
falsification, and focused paired qualification. It reduces expiry-scan key copying, shares
immutable event bytes and tags, transfers RESP output buffers without a redundant copy, bounds
HC/2 queued outbound bytes, and caches the durable-store budget total while preserving its
validating public scan. Management overhead produced a measured no-win; allocator work remains
deferred behind an external profiling blocker; retained-byte admission was not applicable.

The Moka removal observer needed by production accounting is supplied by the independently
published, exact-pinned `hydra-moka 0.12.15-hydra.1` package. Its runtime source is byte-equivalent
to the reviewed Git revision, but the registry package is deliberately treated as a new build
identity. HydraCache does not depend on acceptance of the upstream Moka proposal to publish 0.73.

## Compatibility and claim boundary

The runtime changes preserve public APIs, HC/1, HC/2 and RESP bytes, tagged-key and TTL semantics,
tenant isolation, durable record/checksum/fsync formats, Management Center truth semantics, and
same-disk rollback. The final exact-tree admission checked 44 public package/profile rows against
the published compatibility baseline with `cargo-semver-checks 0.50.0` and Rust 1.94.0; all 44
were classified compatible.

No portable numerical performance improvement is claimed. Five-pair focused host comparisons passed
the frozen goodput, CPU/op, p99, correctness and resource guards for the earlier exact candidate.
Six-hour run `36532416869` and 24-hour replacement confirmation `36839197349` passed every frozen
guard for the exact final registry candidate. In the confirmation, each role completed 1.0368
billion operations and 1,442 checkpoints with zero errors, timeouts or rejections. Goodput was
effectively unchanged, CPU/operation increased 0.260%, p99 improved 0.328%, and the decisive
RSS/anonymous-PSS 95% upper slope bound fell 32.60%. These results admit final C73 but do not claim
portable capacity, allocation savings, or Redis/Hazelcast superiority.

## What the measurements actually showed

The figures below are deliberately split by evidence class. Local allocation and process screens
identify owners and can reject a change, but they are not portable release benchmarks. Only the
dedicated-host rows are release admission evidence, and their fixed 12,000 operations/second load
must not be presented as HydraCache's maximum throughput.

| --- | --- | --- |

`16d2e98b6cc9e22d9ccf95eb26fe28bbbcf80f2b` remained empty. Local final admission ran 3,472 tests
with 3,472 passing and 82 skipped by the reviewed profile; `performance-contract-check

in the W11 record: two exhausted local disk during a cold isolated build/link, and one hit the

passed on the unchanged exact SHA and unchanged limits. None of these infrastructure attempts is
reclassified as product evidence, and neither duration run authorizes a portable RSS, allocation,
capacity, Redis or Hazelcast comparison.

The first final exact-tree admission was PR
[#214](https://github.com/javaquasar/hydracache/pull/214). Its head was the measured candidate
`16d2e98b6cc9e22d9ccf95eb26fe28bbbcf80f2b`; GitHub's synthetic merge commit

[`w11-exact-sha-ci-rejected-37126998956.toml`](https://github.com/javaquasar/hydracache/blob/main/docs/testing/performance/0.73/w11-exact-sha-ci-rejected-37126998956.toml).

`16d2e98b6cc9e22d9ccf95eb26fe28bbbcf80f2b`; therefore the accepted six- and 24-hour product
